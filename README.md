# GEEKS Ethiopia — Digital Health Workforce Development

**National GEEKS Coordinator and Mentor | First Cohort**  
**ICAP at Columbia University in Ethiopia | 2020–2022**

## Project Overview

The Growing Expertise in E-Health Knowledge and Skills (GEEKS) program is a project-based public health informatics capacity-building initiative designed to strengthen digital health skills and support improvements in health information systems.

In Ethiopia, the program involved collaboration among ICAP at Columbia University, the Ethiopian Ministry of Health, the Ethiopian Public Health Institute, and the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC).

## My Role and Contributions

While serving as Senior Data Use Advisor at ICAP in Ethiopia, I served as National GEEKS Coordinator and Mentor for the first cohort.

My responsibilities included:

- Coordinating fellowship training and implementation activities with national public health partners.
- Facilitating stakeholder engagement across participating organizations.
- Supporting applied informatics project development and mentorship arrangements.
- Coordinating follow-up activities, progress reviews, and communication with mentors and fellows.
- Supporting digital health workforce development and the practical use of public health information systems.

## Training and Fellowship Activities

A five-day GEEKS Tier 1 workshop was conducted in Bishoftu, Ethiopia, December 7–11, 2020, with 41 participants.

Four project teams developed initial proposals addressing:

1. Reducing childhood immunization dropout.
2. Optimizing DHIS2 for vaccine-preventable disease reporting.
3. Improving data quality through triangulation.
4. Enhancing access to and use of immunization and surveillance data.

Following training, mentorship and technical review activities supported further project development.

## Broader Program Context

The GEEKS initiative continued beyond my ICAP appointment. ICAP reported in August 2024 that its Ethiopia program had trained more than 150 professionals across three years and supported digital health projects involving DHIS2, disease surveillance, and immunization.

These later outcomes describe the broader program and are not presented as my individual accomplishments.
